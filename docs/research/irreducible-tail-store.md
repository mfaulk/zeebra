# A dense tail store over irreducible monomials: counted, and not prototyped (#1198)

**Status:** Steps 1 and 2 complete. Step 3's gate fails, so no prototype. One follow-up filed
(#1240).
**Measured at:** `74065694` (tag `measured/1198-tail-store-count`), `examples/reuse_census.rs` in
release with `--features research-reuse`, AMD Threadripper PRO 5995WX, Linux x86_64, one worker,
unpinned, one run per row, 2026-11-27. Every figure is a count or a byte total computed from
counts and type sizes; the two peak-RSS figures in §4 are from the same runs, with the count on.
No timing is reported.
**Data:** [`research/1198-irreducible-tail-store/`](../../research/1198-irreducible-tail-store/README.md);
`tables.md` there is generated by `summarize.py`.

Parent evidence: [`m4gb-m5gb-reference.md`](m4gb-m5gb-reference.md) (#1030), whose §9 left open
"Zeebra's own per-degree matrix rows, columns and zero rows on the same shapes".

## 0. Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does Zeebra see the same irreducible monomials M4GB indexes? | **Yes, exactly.** The peak count of matrix columns no basis lead divides is M4GB's peak dense width at all four shapes with counters: 720, 547, 1,321, 1,080 (§2). |
| Would a dense store over them hold 2x fewer live bytes than a batch does now? | **No, against the lane arm the gate names: 1.2–1.9x.** Without the lane, 1.4–2.4x: above 2 at n = 14, below it at n = 16 and n = 18 (§3). |
| Does it lose on sparse tails, as predicted? | **Yes.** Eco-12 0.46x, Katsura-11 0.73x (§3). |
| What does the tail-reduced invariant cost? | **It cannot be kept in the signature engine at all**, and on dense rows 22–74% of stored tail terms are invalidated before the final collapse, against 0.7–3.5% on the controls (§5). |
| Where is the memory, then? | **In the signature basis, not the batch.** At (16, 29) the basis is 89 MiB in term form against a 50 MiB batch peak, of a 150 MiB process (§4). |

**Recommendation: do not build step 3.** The layout's saving in the batch is under the gate on
the rows where memory binds, and its invariant conflicts with signatures. The by-product is
#1240: the signature basis stores 60–84 bytes per term where 8 would do.

## 1. What was counted

`sigf4::tail_store` (behind `research-reuse`, opt-in per run through
`SigF4Options::tail_store_census`) records, per batch of the signature engine:

- the matrix columns, and how many of them no basis lead divides when the batch is built
  (**irreducible columns**) and after its new elements;
- the sparse entries of targets and reducers as built, and the cells of one dense vector per row
  over the irreducible columns (also truncated below each row's lead);
- the distinct reducer multiples `(element, multiplier)` built so far in the run;
- the engine's own live-byte peak for the batch (`LiveBudget`, the quantity `max_live_bytes`
  bounds);
- for each new lead, the stored basis elements holding a tail term the lead newly divides.

It reduces nothing and changes no row: an engine test checks the basis, the product count and
the live-byte peak against a run without it.

Rows: `reuse_census`'s dense quadratic generator (#1031's: each monomial of degree at most two
kept with probability one half, a planted point) at #1030's held-out shapes (14, 25) and (16, 29),
at (14, 28) and (16, 32), and at n = 18, m = 36; GF(31) and GF(32003). Controls: Eco-12 and
MQ11 at 64-bit primes, Katsura-11 at 2³¹ − 1. The generator differs from #1030's
`dense_mq_shaped` only in density, and M4GB's counters depend on the shape alone (#1030 §9).

## 3. Bytes a batch holds

`engine live` is the batch's `LiveBudget` peak. `dense` is one vector per row (targets and
reducers) over the batch's irreducible columns at the field's coefficient size, plus a
column-to-slot map and one accumulator. Both at the batch where each peaks.

| Row | engine live MiB | with the lane MiB | dense MiB | gain | gain against the lane |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (14, 25), GF(31) | 14.9 | 12.3 | 6.6 | 2.26 | **1.87** |
| (14, 25), GF(32003) | 15.4 | — | 6.6 | 2.34 | — |
| (14, 28), GF(31) | 9.9 | — | 5.3 | 1.87 | — |
| (16, 29), GF(31) | 50.0 | 40.1 | 30.3 | 1.65 | **1.33** |
| (16, 29), GF(32003) | 53.4 | 44.0 | 37.3 | 1.43 | **1.18** |
| (16, 32), GF(31) | 40.1 | — | 16.7 | 2.41 | — |
| (18, 36), GF(31) | 120.3 | 98.6 | 70.1 | 1.72 | **1.41** |
| Eco-12 | 30.9 | — | 67.3 | 0.46 | — |
| Katsura-11 | 21.0 | — | 28.7 | 0.73 | — |
| MQ11 (square) | 47.0 | — | 31.7 | 1.48 | — |

- **The gate is 2x on the dense rows, against the route with the lane on. It fails on all four
  lane rows**, and the gain falls as n grows, which is the direction in which budgets bind.
- **The dense figure is a floor for the layout, not an estimate of a working store.** It charges
  only the batch's own rows. A tail-reduced reducer also needs the lower multiples it was reduced
  by; charging every distinct multiple of the run one vector gives 38.3 MiB at (16, 29) and
  85.9 MiB at (18, 36), a gain of 1.05–1.4x. M4GB's store peaks at 19,197 multiples at (16, 29)
  against the 7,591 Zeebra builds.
- **Truncating each vector below its lead buys 3–12%**, so it does not change the answer.
- **What would change it is a narrower coefficient**, which a dense vector takes in full and a
  sparse `(u32, F)` entry does not. At one byte per GF(31) coefficient the (16, 29) store is
  7.6 MiB. That is #1078's question (coefficient and index width), and it is separable from the
  irreducible indexing.
- **No dispatch rule was derived.** The obvious property, built entries over dense cells, is
  0.25–1.12 on the dense overdetermined rows and 0.21–0.48 on the controls, so it does not
  separate them. Nothing is being dispatched, so none is needed.

## 4. Where the memory is

| Row | peak RSS MiB (count on) | batch live MiB | basis terms | bytes per term | basis MiB | basis as dense vectors MiB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (14, 25), GF(31) | 43 | 14.9 | 306,231 | 68 | 19.9 | 1.2 |
| (16, 29), GF(31) | 150 | 50.0 | 1,232,450 | 76 | 89.3 | 5.6 |
| (18, 36), GF(31) | 340 | 120.3 | 2,522,668 | 84 | 202.1 | 10.9 |
| Eco-12 | 134 | 30.9 | 1,050,870 | 64 | 64.1 | 15.3 |
| Katsura-11 | 86 | 21.0 | 392,682 | 60 | 22.5 | 4.3 |

- **The signature basis is the larger holder on every row**: 1.1–2.1x the batch peak. Each term
  is a full `Monomial<N>` (4N bytes), a coefficient and an 8-byte additive hash
  (`Element::terms` and `Element::hashes` in `sigf4/engine.rs`).
- Every one of those monomials is already interned in the run's `MonomialTable`, which holds the
  monomial and its hash. A term stored as a table id and a coefficient is 8 bytes at a 32-bit
  field: 9.4 MiB instead of 89.3 at (16, 29). That needs no tail reduction and no dense index.
  It is #1240.
- This is the part of M4GB's 16–17 MiB against Zeebra's 148–164 MiB (#1030 §8) that the batch
  comparison of §3 cannot reach. It is also outside `max_live_bytes`, which charges batches only,
  so it does not bear on #1186's stops.

## 5. The cost of the invariant

M4GB keeps every stored tail irreducible, so a new lead forces every stored polynomial with a
tail term it divides to be reduced again.

| Row | tail terms | reducible when added | invalidated later | invalidated before the last adding batch | new leads that invalidate | stored elements touched per such lead |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (14, 25) | 305,338 | 0.0% | 99.7% | 30.1% | 885 of 893 | 344 |
| (14, 28) | 181,518 | 0.0% | 99.6% | 74.3% | 675 of 687 | 268 |
| (16, 29) | 1,230,644 | 0.0% | 99.9% | 22.3% | 1,799 of 1,806 | 683 |
| (16, 32) | 643,933 | 0.0% | 99.8% | 37.9% | 1,264 of 1,272 | 509 |
| (18, 36) | 2,520,075 | 0.0% | 99.9% | 25.7% | 2,584 of 2,593 | 974 |
| Eco-12 | 1,048,986 | 1.7% | 3.5% | 3.4% | 437 of 1,884 | 78 |
| Katsura-11 | 392,128 | 0.0% | 0.7% | 0.7% | 344 of 554 | 7.5 |
| MQ11 (square) | 1,266,994 | 0.0% | 3.4% | 3.4% | 756 of 767 | 57 |

- **Elements are tail-reduced when added** (0.0%): the engine already clears every admissible
  pivot column. What it does not do is go back.
- **On dense overdetermined rows nearly every new lead invalidates a third of the store.** A
  planted row ends in a collapse to linear leads, which invalidates everything; that batch is
  excluded in the fifth column because nothing reads the store after it. Before it, 22–74% of the
  stored tail terms have been invalidated, against 0.7–3.5% on the controls. The dense rows are
  where the layout fits and where the invariant is most expensive.
- **These are lower bounds.** A term is counted once, when its monomial first becomes reducible.
  The terms a re-reduction would add are not followed.
- **The signature engine cannot do the re-reduction.** Batches run in increasing signature, so a
  later element has a signature at least as large as every stored one, and so does any multiple
  of it. Reducing a stored tail by it would raise the stored element's signature, which the
  rewrite and syzygy criteria depend on. The invariant belongs to an engine without signatures.
  Zeebra's adaptive route sends these rows to the signature engine because it is 2.2–3.5x faster
  on them than conventional F4 (#1030 §8).

## 6. What this settles for #1030 §6

#1030 could not split M4GB's 5.8–16x over Zeebra between M4GB's organization and Zeebra's
arithmetic. The counts say how much of the organization is transferable:

- the **index set** is the same in both engines (§2), so it is not an algorithmic advantage;
- the **batch memory** it saves is 1.2–1.9x against the lane (§3);
- the **process memory** gap is mostly basis term storage (§4), which is a representation choice
  in Zeebra's signature engine unrelated to M4GB.

Nothing here measures time. Zeebra builds 9,702 reducer rows at (16, 29), 7,591 distinct, and
22% of them repeat an earlier batch's multiple, which is #1031's and #1075's subject.

One count is worth restating from the statistics these runs carry: 76–86% of the dense rows'
elimination products are spent on rows that reduce to zero, and the batch after the collapse adds
no element (4,434 targets and 3,013 reducers at (16, 29)). #946 is that question.

## 7. Reopening conditions

- A conventional-F4 route that is competitive with the signature engine on dense overdetermined
  rows, where the invariant can be kept.
- #1078 landing a one- or two-byte coefficient: then recount §3 with dense vectors at that width
  against sparse entries at theirs.
- A batch budget that binds on a row where §3's gain is above 2 (so far only n = 14 and (16, 32)
  without the lane, which run in 10–40 MiB).

## 8. Limits

- One run per row. The counts are deterministic; the (14, 25) and (16, 29) rows were repeated on a
  second support and a second field with the same widths.
- The dense rows are planted. An unplanted row ends at `1` instead of at linear leads; #1030 §9
  reports M4GB's counters equal up to the last round.
- The count is in the signature engine only. Conventional F4's matrices on these rows were not
  counted.
- Peak RSS was read with the count on, which adds its own ledger; it is context for §4's byte
  totals, not a measurement of the shipping route.
