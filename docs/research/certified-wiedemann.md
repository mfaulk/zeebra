## Algorithm and certificate

The algorithmic reference is [Faugère and Mou, Sparse FGLM algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.1238).
This implementation uses a scalar projection of multiplication by the lex-last variable. It does
not import another engine's source or implement block Wiedemann, coordinate changes, or radical-only
reconstruction.

For quotient dimension `D`, form `2D` scalar moments using the transpose multiplication operator.
Coordinate classes are sparse moment queries. Berlekamp–Massey supplies a monic recurrence; only
full degree `D` proceeds. From the polynomial part of the recurrence times each moment series,
modular inversion reconstructs the coordinate polynomials. Classical polynomial arithmetic keeps
this prototype quadratic in its scalar work; no fast Hankel solver is claimed.

No random result crosses the API boundary unchecked. The independent shape certificate evaluates
every reconstructed relation in the quotient algebra, using right operator applications rather
than scalar moments. It shares a single sequence of powers across relations, without sharing any
projection or recurrence data. Membership plus the shape leading monomials and matching quotient
dimension proves that the result generates the original ideal. Construction makes each relation
monic with reduced tails. Nonradical factors are retained.

Three fixed-seed projections bound retries. Exhaustion means either unlucky projections or a
nonseparating variable; the report does not conflate those. Failed verification and insufficient
workspace also select classical FGLM. Statistics record the route, attempts and fallback reason;
the external adapter stores the strategy in its version and the actual selection in stage metadata.

## Routing and budgets

`recommended()` uses `ConversionStrategy::Adaptive`. Dimensions below 128 and last operators
scanning more than one quarter of a dense matrix stay classical. This is a conservative routing
heuristic, not a mathematical shape test or a universal crossover theorem. `baseline()` and
`unbounded()` retain classical conversion for reproducible ablations. `Wiedemann` forces the bounded
attempt for tests and measurements, with the same certificate and fallback.

The attempt checks `(4N + 64)(D + 1)` allowance cells, and a conservative byte allowance including
index storage, against the existing span cell and byte limits before allocating. It reports these
separately from classical quadratic blocks. Fast workspace is released before fallback allocates
its dense blocks. Operator limits and the quotient enumeration cap still apply. These bounds
exclude output polynomial maps and allocator overhead; they are not process-RSS limits.

The family below has generators `x_i^5-x_(i+1)` and `x_(N-1)^5-x_0-1` over `GF(65537)`. Their
pairwise coprime leading monomials make them a grevlex basis, with quotient dimension `5^N`.
The last variable generates the quotient: its fifth power recovers `x_0`, and successive fifth
powers recover the others. This supplies reproducible sparse shape-position workloads of several
sizes without timing basis construction. It is not evidence that every ideal is in shape position.

## Public paired measurements

Retained samples, source/output digests and RSS are in
[`wiedemann-368.json`](../../research/fglm/wiedemann-368.json). All three repetitions in each
comparison returned exactly equal reduced lex bases, and all adaptive attempts certified their
first projection. Times include operator construction; its column below is the adaptive median.

| variables | quotient dimension | classical median | adaptive median | end-to-end ratio | operator construction |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 625 | 54.138 ms | 12.641 ms | 4.28x | 0.389 ms |
| 5 | 3 125 | 5 984.120 ms | 251.913 ms | 23.75x | 3.078 ms |

These are within-process, alternating-order comparisons, not ratios to a historical session.
Three repetitions give a mechanism check, not a universal factor. The family is deliberately
sparse and in shape position; other families can spend most of their time constructing operators,
or have no separating last variable at all. Adaptive fallback and the unchanged classical path
remain necessary.

At dimension 3 125, fresh one-route processes measured **161 603 584 bytes** peak RSS for classical
and **5 660 672 bytes** for adaptive. These are measured self high-water marks, not values inferred
from the workspace allowance. The comparison process's higher peak is not used for this ratio.

msolve 0.11.0 returned an exactly equal monic eliminant on both inputs. Its process wall was
**11.596 ms** at dimension 625 and **476.578 ms** at dimension 3 125; measured peak RSS was
**5 013 504** and **19 050 496 bytes**, respectively. A separate degree-five one-variable run took
**11.605 ms**, so the first external comparison is at the startup floor and is disqualified.
The larger run clears that floor, but it is one external attempt, and Zeebra's full certified-lex
construction and msolve's parametrization have different internal work. This is an exact task-level
eliminant cross-check, not a general engine-parity claim or a replacement for the competitive board.

## Reproducing

Build before acquiring the measurement lock. The example alternates route order and asserts the
full reduced lex bases equal on every repeat. Its printed source and output digests identify the
actual polynomials; the source digest is over the canonical displayed basis, not its path.

```sh
mkdir -p /buildcache/cargo-target/zeebra-368/tmp
CARGO_TARGET_DIR=/buildcache/cargo-target/zeebra-368 \
TMPDIR=/buildcache/cargo-target/zeebra-368/tmp RUSTC_WRAPPER='' \
  cargo build --locked --release --example wiedemann_conversion -j16
(
  ulimit -v 16777216
  ZEEBRA_MACHINE=5995wx ZEEBRA_AFFINITY=2 \
  TMPDIR=/buildcache/cargo-target/zeebra-368/tmp \
    tools/benchlock.sh wiedemann-368 timeout --signal=INT --kill-after=30s 600 \
    taskset -c 2 /buildcache/cargo-target/zeebra-368/release/examples/wiedemann_conversion \
    --variables 5 --degree 5 --repeats 3
)
```

Use separate `--route classical` and `--route adaptive` processes with one repeat for attributable
RSS. The comparison process's high-water mark spans both routes and is not their memory ratio.
Optional `--msolve PATH` drives the existing adapter at one worker, verifies exact equality of the
monic eliminant, and prints process wall, stage reports and measured RSS. Zeebra constructs a full
certified lex basis before projecting its eliminant; msolve performs its own basis pass on the same
prepared generators. Small process timings still need the existing startup-floor screen.

## Limits and negative results

The first target-sized comparison improved conversion while repeated relation-by-relation
certification still dominated avoidable work. Sharing quotient powers removes those repeated
matvecs without weakening the certificate; the current measurements use that implementation.
A reconstruction's scalar recurrence alone is not a certificate, even at full degree.

A nonseparating coordinate can incur three full scalar sequences before falling back, so adaptive
selection is not guaranteed faster on every sparse ideal. The density/size heuristic does not
replace a shape-position characterization, and low-degree coordinates remain a tuning opportunity.

Operator construction is unchanged and can refuse before either conversion route runs. Raising a
span budget cannot resolve a border reducer-entry stop; the example exposes that budget separately
for deliberate bounded measurements. A faster conversion does not resolve an earlier F4 refusal.

No all-field speedup, cryptanalytic frontier extension, or universal parity with msolve is claimed.
The numerical policy evidence above concerns this public family; target-specific research stays
with its instruments after #668. Small-field and nonradical correctness, fallback behavior,
workspace refusals and independent tamper rejection are automated tests, not timing claims.
