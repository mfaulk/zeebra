## 2. Where the time goes (medians, ms)

Zeebra's figures are the stage clocks of the arm that runs `entry`'s route. msolve's come from its
own `-v 2` timings at 10 ms resolution. Its process wall adds about 24 ms for start-up and output.

**MQ10 / GF(2³¹−1) at 4 w** (msolve's best count; gap 158 ms of process wall):

| Stage | Zeebra (`f4stages`) | msolve (`-v 2`) | Zeebra − msolve |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| select, symbolic, construction (msolve: select + symbolic + update + convert) | 54.8 | 70 | −15 |
| elimination: forward 208.0 + backward 16.5 (msolve: linear algebra) | 224.5 | 80 | **+145** |
| extraction, interreduction, unstaged (msolve: reduce gb) | 34.7 | 0 | +35 |
| solver call | 313.7 | 150 | +164 |
| process wall | 332.0 | 174.0 | +158 |

**MQ10 / GF(32003) at 8 w** (gap 145 ms): elimination is 154.8 ms (forward 137.7, backward 17.1)
against msolve's **20 ms**, +135. Symbolic and construction are 51 ms against 70.

**MQ10 at 1 w** (the signature route; anomalous for msolve under §8.4, so not scored): elimination
is level with msolve's (170 against 170 ms on GF(2³¹−1)), and the gap is symbolic preprocessing,
197 ms against msolve's 70. The signature route applies only 83 M products, 0.29× msolve's.

**Katsura-11 / GF(2³¹−1) at 8 w** (the primary; Zeebra leads by 214 ms):

| Stage | Zeebra (`sigf4claim`) | msolve | Zeebra − msolve |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| bookkeeping (msolve: select + update + convert) | 16.8 | 130 | −113 |
| symbolic | 170.0 | 170 | 0 |
| elimination | 77.6 | 280 | −202 |
| interreduction (msolve: reduce gb) | 114.9 | 0 | **+115** |
| solver call | 411.9 | 600 | −188 |
| process wall | 497.0 | 711.3 | −214 |

The lead is the signature route's work count: **200 M coefficient products against msolve's 2,744 M**
(16.8 M pivot applications of 164 products each), at every worker count. Zeebra's remaining cost is
serial. Symbolic (170 ms, 34.3% [33.3, 34.7] of the wall) and interreduction (115 ms, **23.1% [22.8,
23.9]**) do not shrink from 1 to 16 w. Elimination does: 346 → 119 → 78 → 67 ms. msolve's final
reduction is free because its basis is already reduced when its last round ends.

## 3. HM1: work count, then per-product cost

| Row | w | Zeebra products (fwd + bwd) | msolve products | **ratio** | Zeebra row ops / msolve applications | products per op (Zeebra / msolve) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MQ10 / GF(2³¹−1) | 4 | 515.0 + 23.5 M | 281.3 M | **1.91** | 7.42 / 5.35 M | 69.4 / 52.5 |
| | 16 | 506.9 + 23.2 M | 281.4 M | 1.88 | 7.41 / 5.35 M | 68.4 / 52.5 |
| MQ10 / GF(32003) | 8 | 511.9 + 23.4 M | 281.4 M | 1.90 | 7.42 / 5.35 M | 69.0 / 52.5 |
| Katsura-11 / GF(2³¹−1) | 8 | 200.1 M (signature route) | 2,744.0 M | 0.07 | — | — |

The 1-w rows and the full table are in `tables.md`. msolve's counts come from its exact sparse
linear algebra (`tools/external/msolve-count.py`, untimed). They change by under 0.1% with the
thread count.

**HM1 is falsified on MQ10**: the products are 1.9×, over the 1.5× threshold. Of the 2.8×
elimination-time ratio at 4 w on GF(2³¹−1), work count explains 1.91× and per-thread cost the rest.
That cost is 1.51 ns per product per thread for Zeebra, against at most 1.14 for msolve (an upper
bound, since it assumes every msolve thread is busy throughout its linear algebra). On GF(32003)
per-product cost is the larger factor: Zeebra is 1.85–1.99 ns, *slower* than on GF(2³¹−1), against
msolve's ≤ 0.57 ns, where msolve uses its 16-bit kernel. Zeebra's canonical u64 remainder costs
more than `Mersenne31`'s shift-and-add.

**The work excess is not the matrices' size.** The fewest-terms reducer rule (session `rd`) gives
Zeebra msolve-shaped matrices: 10,569 × 10,198 at the largest against msolve's 9,883 × 10,226, and
2.35 M entries over all rounds against msolve's 2.41 M (session `rd`). Under the default rule the
largest is 12,391 × 12,020 with 2.52 M entries. Yet fewest-terms cuts the products by only 8%. The
FL order then removes Zeebra's inherited reducer-column entries, bringing products per application
to 53.3, level with msolve's 52.5. Even both together leave **1.55× msolve's products and 1.43× its
pivot applications** (7.63 M against 5.35 M) on near-identical inputs. That residual is in the
elimination trajectory, the order in which rows meet pivots and fill in. This study did not locate
it any further. A per-round count on both sides is M2's first diagnostic if the owner chooses MQ10
(§8).

## 4. HM3: scaling and the critical path

Conventional route, MQ10 / GF(2³¹−1). Imbalance is the slowest worker ÷ the mean worker. Utilization
is worker time ÷ (workers × forward wall).

| w | call | forward | critical path | imbalance | utilization | lock + publish | retry products | ns/product/thread | serial stages |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 313.7 | 208.0 | 196.1 | 1.01 | 94% | 0.18 | 1.9% | 1.51 | 76.0 |
| 8 | 214.6 | 113.4 | 101.1 | 1.02 | 88% | 0.20 | 4.4% | 1.55 | 73.4 |
| 16 | 192.0 | 68.5 | 55.3 | 0.98 | 82% | 0.40 | 8.9% | 1.64 | 91.5 |

The serial stages are symbolic, construction, extraction and interreduction. GF(32003) has the same
shape (`tables.md`).

- **The forward pass is not bandwidth-bound.** Per-product cost rises only 8% from 4 to 16 w.
  Workers are balanced to 2%, and lock and publication time is under 0.5 ms. The losses at 16 w are
  retries (8.9% of products are reapplied after a lost publication) and 18% of the workers' slot
  time idle inside the pass.
- **The critical path at 16 w is the serial stages.** They take 91.5 ms (symbolic grows from 35 to
  47 ms at 16 w), backward 15 ms and unstaged work 17 ms. Together that is 64% of the 192 ms call,
  against 68.5 ms of forward. HM3's falsifier (scaling flat while the serial share is under 10%)
  does not hold.
- **On the primary**, Katsura-11 at 16 w, symbolic, interreduction and bookkeeping are 315 of
  421 ms (75%), and process CPU ÷ wall is 2.0. msolve's 16-w slowdown (2,614 ms) is its linear
  algebra: 2,020 ms elapsed and 35 s of CPU, which is spinning. That is the anomaly §8.4 excludes.

## 5. HM2: delayed reduction below 2¹⁶

- **Where it could pay.** Only MQ10 / GF(32003) qualifies among the development rows, where
  Zeebra's per-product cost is ≥ 3.4× msolve's. #269's pilot cut arithmetic by 28% there, and the
  forward pass is 57% of the 8-w call. That bounds the gain near 16% of the call before any gate.
- **What it serves.** That row's msolve median is 116 ms at 8 w, under the 300 ms floor, so it
  cannot score a win at any Zeebra time (§11.6). Both candidate target rows (§8) are at GF(2³¹−1),
  where #270 closed delayed reduction.
- **What was not done.** The #269 crossover was not re-run on current code. Its overlay is against
  `6578c0ab` and would need porting, which is a candidate implementation, not attribution. #945's
  dense GF(31) forward pass is the same mechanism at a smaller prime, and its 93% forward share says
  a gated kernel would matter there. Neither is on the M33 scoreboard. HM2 therefore stays
  **untested** and is not one of the two M2 candidates.

## 9. Coverage: measurements the issue lists that are missing

- **Memory traffic.** Not measured: `perf` is not installed on this host and
  `perf_event_paranoid` is 2. Installing it is an owner action. The bandwidth question (HM3) is
  answered by per-product cost against worker count (§4) instead.
- **Peak RSS** (`wait4`, an upper bound for msolve until #990). Zeebra uses 40 MiB on MQ10 and
  110–113 MiB on Katsura-11; msolve 21 and 29 MiB. Neither changes with workers.
- **Time per useful reduction** is not reported separately. Zeebra and msolve reduce the same
  number of rows to zero on MQ10 (1,528 against 1,530), so the products ratio is also the ratio per
  useful row.
- **Matrix dimensions per batch** are reported for msolve (its `-v 2` rounds are in the records).
  Zeebra reports only the largest matrix and totals, since `F4Statistics` keeps no per-batch log.
- **Worker utilization on the signature route** (Katsura-11) is available only as process CPU ÷
  wall (1.75 at 8 w). The cascade's own busy-time clocks are not part of this instrument.
- **msolve's thread counts** are #966's census: exactly `-t` at 1/4/8/16.

## 10. Instrumentation

All of it is behind `F4Options::measure_stages` or confined to the study binary: 130 net lines in
`src/` (two tests included) and 87 in `examples/sig_f4_study.rs`, within the 400-line cap:

- `ParallelForwardStatistics` gains passes, worker slots, busy time, the critical path, scheduling
  and publication time, products and retry products. They are filled only when stages are measured,
  which also turns on the concurrent kernel's existing per-row clocks (`Config::measure_work`).
- `StageTimings` gains `fl_sweep` and `fl_echelon` (#918).
- `sig_f4_study` gains `--stages`, `--fl` and `--reducer`.

There is no other library behaviour change. Overhead is in §1.
