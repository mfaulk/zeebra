## 0. Verdict

| Question | Answer |

| Prototype the Hermite-normal-form conversion? | **No-go.** No row above a second reaches the 1.5x gate (§5). |

## 1. What the paper proves, and under what assumptions

Notation: `R = K[x_1, …, x_{n−1}, y]`, with `y` the smallest variable in both orders; `I`
zero-dimensional of degree `D`; `B` the grevlex monomial basis of `R/I`. In Zeebra's indexing `y` is
variable `N − 1`, the "last" variable of `fglm::shape` and of the Wiedemann route.

- **Shape position** (Eq. 1). The reduced lex basis is `{h(y), x_{n−1} − g_{n−1}(y), …, x_1 −
  g_1(y)}`, `deg h = D`.
- **Stability** (Definition 2.1), a property of the grevlex leading monomials alone: for every
  leading monomial `μ` of `I` divisible by `y` and every other variable `x_i`, `x_i μ / y` is again
  a leading monomial of `I`. Lemma 2.2: it is enough to check the minimal generators.
- **The module.** Take `T = (μ_1, …, μ_t)`, the standard monomials not divisible by `y`, and
  `M = I ∩ (K[y] μ_1 + … + K[y] μ_t)`. `M` is a free `K[y]`-module of rank `t` (Lemma 3.4).
- **Theorem 5.2**, with no stability assumption: let `e_i` be the length of the `y`-chain of
  standard monomials above `μ_i`, so `Σ e_i = D`. Then `{y^{e_i} μ_i − nf(y^{e_i} μ_i)}` is a basis
  of `M`, and as a `t × t` matrix over `K[y]` it is `P = diag(y^{e_i}) + R` with column `j` of `R`
  of degree below `e_j`. `P` is the compressed form of `y·1 − M_y`, where `M_y` is the `D × D`
  multiplication operator: `t` normal forms build it, the same `t` that are `M_y`'s non-unit columns.
- **Corollary 5.4**, under stability: those `t` polynomials are exactly the elements of the reduced
  grevlex basis whose leading monomial is divisible by `y`. `P` is read off the basis. No normal
  form is computed.
- **Theorem 4.1**: if the lex basis lies in `K[y] μ_1 + … + K[y] μ_t`, it is read off the rows of
  the Hermite normal form `H` of `P`. In shape position that holds when `x_1, …, x_{n−1}` are
  standard (Lemma 5.1), and `H` then has diagonal `(h, 1, …, 1)`.
- **Theorem 1.1**: the conversion costs `Õ(t^{ω−1} D)` field operations, against `O(t D²)` for the
  sparse-FGLM/Wiedemann route when `M_y`'s `t` columns are dense.

Stability and shape position are used independently. Shape position can be detected after the fact
from the degrees in `H` (§6.1 of the paper), so the route is deterministic and needs no certificate.

**The implementation the paper measures** (§6.1) does not compute `H` whole. It computes a kernel
basis `K` of the right `t × (t − n)` block of `P` (Zhou–Labahn–Storjohann), multiplies `K` into the
left `n` columns, and takes the Hermite form of the resulting `n × n` matrix. The stated cost is
`O(t^ω M(D/t) log(D/t))` for the kernel basis. It is built on PML and NTL: fast matrix
multiplication over `Z/p`, FFT polynomial arithmetic and approximant bases.

## 2. Testing the assumptions cheaply

`grobner::fglm::module_census(leading, standard)` computes everything §1 needs from the leading
monomials and the standard monomials, with no field arithmetic:

| Field | Meaning | Paper |
|---|---|---|
| `module_rank` | `t`, the order of `P` | Theorem 5.2 |
| `column_degrees` | the `e_i`, ascending | Theorem 5.2 |
| `last_variable_leaders` | minimal leading monomials divisible by `y`; equals `t` when stable | Corollary 5.4 |
| `stable` | `S(lt(I))`, checked on minimal generators | Definition 2.1, Lemma 2.2 |
| `other_variables_standard` | `x_1, …, x_{n−1} ∈ B` | Lemma 5.1 |

Stability costs `O(|G|² N)` monomial comparisons after the quotient basis exists. Shape position
has no cheap test before converting; Zeebra's Wiedemann route already learns it from the recurrence
degree and certifies it.

`lex_pipeline --arm probe` prints the census beside the operator shape. The Katsura and dense
quadratic rows were cross-checked against a separate Python census over Singular's `std` leading
exponents (Katsura-6, 9, 10, 11; six, eight and ten dense quadratics): identical `D`, `t`, stability
and longest chain.

| `pquad-12-1` | 12 | 4,096 | 924 | 924 | yes | yes | 4.4 | 3,567,259 | 0.94 |

Three shapes, and they do not overlap:

- **Chain rows are the opposite of stable.** The generators `x_i^d − x_{i+1}` have a box staircase:
  one leading monomial is divisible by `y`, and `t = D / d` standard monomials are free of it. `P`
  is `y^d·1 − R` of order `D / d`. The operator is not dense either: 0.07–0.33 stored entries per
  column.
- **Katsura and the planted quadratics are the paper's regime**: stable, `t = D / 3` to `D / 4.4`,
  and the `t` non-unit columns are 77–95% full. `pquad-N` has `t = C(N, N/2)`, the paper's
  `rand N, 2` row exactly. On Katsura `x_0` is a leading monomial (the linear generator), so
  `other_variables_standard` is false; `x_0`'s relation then follows from that generator and the
  other `g_i`, not from `H`.
- **`cyclic-7` is stable and not in shape position.** Stability and shape position are
  independent in practice as well as in the paper, and Theorem 4.1 needs the second. The
  non-shape chain controls (`wdns`, `wdnr`) were not censused.

## 4. Cost model

Field operations, one worker. Scalar route as shipped (`fglm/wiedemann.rs`): `2D` transposed
applications of the last operator for the sequence, `D` applications plus `(N − 1) D²` for the
shared-power certificate, so `3 D (z + u) + (N − 1) D²`. Reconstruction is left out: it is
negligible on the dense rows and about half the wall on the chain rows, so the scalar count there
is low by about 2x. Polynomial-matrix route: `t² D`, which is the paper's `t^{ω−1} D` at `ω = 3` with
every constant and logarithm dropped. That makes the second column a **floor**, and the ratio a
ceiling on the gain.

| Row | scalar: sequence + certificate | polynomial-matrix floor | scalar ÷ floor (ceiling on gain) | paper, same (D, t) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `wd-4-10` | 6.3e8 | 1.0e10 | 0.06 | — |
| `wd-3-30` | 3.7e9 | 2.2e10 | 0.17 | — |
| `wdsq-4-10` | 2.6e9 | 2.0e10 | 0.13 | — |
| `wd-6-6` | 1.8e10 | 2.8e12 | 0.01 | — |
| `katsura-11` | 8.4e8 | 1.2e8 | 7.0 | — |
| `katsura-12` | 6.5e9 | 9.6e8 | 6.8 | — |
| `pquad-10-1` | 7.6e8 | 6.5e7 | 11.7 | — |
| `pquad-12-1` | 4.4e10 | 3.5e9 | 12.6 | 1.23 measured |

- **Chain rows: the floor is 6–150x above the scalar route's count** (3–75x with reconstruction
  charged). `t = D / d` puts
  `t² D = D³ / d²` against `≈ 3 D²`. The route would win only if `(D/d)^{ω−1} < D`, that is
  `ω < 2.33` at `wd-4-10` and `ω < 2.5` at `wd-3-30`. No implemented multiplication is there.
- **Dense rows: a ceiling of 7–13x, of which the paper realises a tenth.** At `(D, t) = (4096,
  924)`, the paper's `rand 12, 2` and Zeebra's `pquad-12-1`, the model's ceiling is 12.6 and the
  paper's measured msolve-Wiedemann ÷ Hermite ratio is 1.23. At `(2048, 462)` it is 1.5. It reaches
  3.0 at `D = 8192` and 5.2 at `D = 65536`. The dropped constants are an order of magnitude at the
  sizes Zeebra can reach.
- **Why the ceiling is not reachable here.** On these rows `D / t ≈ 3–4.4`, so `P` is a large
  matrix of tiny degree and the polynomial arithmetic in `y` contributes nothing: `M(4) ≈ 4²`.
  A cubic algorithm (a Mulders–Storjohann weak Popov form, or an iterative order basis) costs
  `t³ (D/t)² = t D²`, the same order as the scalar route. The whole of Theorem 1.1's advantage on
  this shape is `t^{3−ω}`, and it exists only with sub-cubic matrix multiplication and a kernel-basis
  algorithm that reduces to it. Zeebra has neither: no Strassen-class product in `src/linalg`
  (#1193 is researching one), and no polynomial-matrix arithmetic at all (#689).

### One criterion for all three shapes

With `δ = D / t` the mean chain length:

| Shape | `t` against `δ` | Best route | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| chain (`wd-*`) | `t ≫ δ`, operator sparse | scalar Wiedemann, or lex F4 where the ideal is radical | `z ≪ t D`; the matrix order is the problem |
| generic dense (Katsura, `pquad`) | `t ≫ δ ≈ 4`, operator dense | scalar Wiedemann today; Hermite only with `ω < 3` kernels and `t` in the thousands | gain is `t^{3−ω}` and nothing else |
| Griffin / FreeLunch | `t ≪ δ` (`t = 9…81`, `δ = 10³…5·10⁴`) | evaluation–interpolation determinant, `D t³ / 3` | within `t / 3` of the `t² D` floor, from code Zeebra has |

The scalar route costs about `9 D / t² = 9 δ / t` times the evaluation sweep, so the sweep wins
exactly when chains are long and few. No row of the lex panel is in that regime.

## 5. The gate

The issue's gate is a predicted gain of at least 1.5x on a row above a second. Stage medians are
#1039's `zb-wiedemann` arm (ms, one worker, publication mode).

| Row | wall | F4 | operators | sequence + reconstruction + certificate | wall without operators | row gain if the scalar stages were free | gain needed on them for 1.5x |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `katsura-11` | 3,767 | 505 | 2,212 | 986 | 1,554 | 2.73 | **2.11** |
| `pquad-10-1` | 2,080 | 557 | 683 | 793 | 1,397 | 2.31 | **2.42** |

The Hermite route replaces sequence, reconstruction and certificate. It also needs no operator, but
that saving belongs to §6.1 and is available to the scalar route too, so it is taken first.

- `katsura-11`: the scalar stages must get **2.11x** faster to move the row 1.5x; `pquad-10-1`
  **2.42x**. If they were free the rows would gain 2.7x and 2.3x.
- The paper's implementation measures 1.2–1.5x at `D = 2048–4096`, with fast matrix multiplication
  and FFT arithmetic behind it. A first Zeebra implementation on cubic linear algebra is predicted
  at or below 1x (§4).
- Larger rows do not rescue it. At `D = 8192` the paper's ratio is 3.0, but its F4 stage is 970 s
  against 104 s of conversion; in Zeebra `pquad-12-1` already spends 45.7 s in grevlex F4
  (development figure, `wiedemann-pipeline-vs-grobx.md` §3) and `pquad-13` is not on any panel.
- Chain rows are above a second and conversion is all of their cost, but the route loses there by
  §4's floor.

**No-go.** The prototype is not started.

## 6. What is new relative to `order-conversion.md`

That record lists eight alternatives (A–H) and a deferred item. This is a ninth.

**J. A polynomial-matrix (Hermite) conversion — no.** It attacks the right stage (the scalar
sequence and certificate, not border reduction), it is deterministic, and its assumptions hold on
Katsura and planted quadratics. It fails on cost at reachable sizes, and it does not apply to the
family where conversion is the whole cost. `order-conversion.md` §5.F already quotes FreeLunch on
the stability property; it did not test stability on any Zeebra row, and §3 here does.

### 6.1 The last operator is the basis, and the only operator the Wiedemann route uses

This is Corollary 5.4 read for the route Zeebra ships, and it is the finding worth acting on.

- `wiedemann::reconstruct` applies `algebra.operator(N − 1)` and nothing else; the other variables
  enter through `multiply_by_variable(i, one)`, which is `nf(x_i)`. `shape_relations_vanish` raises
  powers of the same operator. **No other operator is read.**
- `QuotientAlgebra::new` builds all `N` operators by border reduction before the route is chosen.
  The last one is 3.9–4.6% of all stored operator entries on these rows (`katsura-11`: 269,867 of
  5,927,377; `pquad-12-1`: 3,567,259 of 91,199,902).
- On a stable staircase every non-unit column of the last operator is `y μ = lt(g)` for a basis
  element `g`, so the column is `−tail(g)`: a copy, with no reduction. Without stability it is `t`
  normal forms, still a fraction of the border.

What that is worth, from §5's medians:

| Row | Wiedemann arm | operators | arm without them | `adaptive` today | grobx |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `katsura-11` | 3,767 | 2,212 (59%) | 1,554 | 3,357 (classical) | 1,213 |
| `pquad-10-1` | 2,080 | 683 (33%) | 1,397 | 2,080 (Wiedemann) | 883 |

Predicted, not measured: 2.2x on `katsura-11` and 1.5x on `pquad-10-1` against today's default,
which would bring those rows from 2.8x and 2.4x behind grobx to about 1.3x and 1.6x. Two things
follow and belong to the follow-up, not here: the `DenseOperator` rule (`D² / 4`) was calibrated
when both routes paid for every operator, and sends `katsura-11` to the classical walk; and the
classical fallback after a failed projection would have to build the remaining operators then.

### 6.2 A hypothesis about the sequence kernel, not a measurement

The paper times msolve's Wiedemann at 6.5 s on `(D, t) = (4096, 924)` over a 30-bit prime. Zeebra's
sequence and certificate on `pquad-12-1`, the same shape over a 17-bit prime, took 98 s in one
unreserved development attempt. Different hosts, different primes, different load; the ratio is not
a result. It is a reason to look: on these rows a transposed application is `t` dense dot products
of length `D`, which is the shape #1183's delayed-reduction lane and #1196's bands are built for,
and `block-krylov-conversion.md` §5 names "a field where lanes pay" as its reopening trigger.
Passed to #1188 as a note.

### 6.3 Griffin

FreeLunch's Lemma 2 matrix, `x_0^{α_0}·1 + Σ x_0^i M_i` of order `D_H`, has the form Theorem 5.2
gives for `T` the standard monomials free of `x_0`: all column degrees equal `α_0 = D_I / D_H`.
`matGen` is the `t = D_H` normal forms of that theorem, with no Corollary 5.4 shortcut because the
staircase is not stable (`griffin-freelunch.md` §4.2). So the paper adds no capability there, and
its Hermite form buys nothing over the determinant sweep at `D_H ≤ 81` (§4's table).
`griffin-freelunch.md` §4.1's conclusion and #689's deferral are unchanged.

## 7. Reopening conditions

All three, in this order:

1. #1233 lands, so that what remains of a dense row is the scalar stages and F4.
2. Zeebra has a dense product over `Z/p` with a measured effective exponent below 3 at order
   `t ≥ 1000` (#1193).
3. A registered row with a stable staircase, shape position and `D ≥ 8192` spends more than 60% of
   its wall in the scalar stages after (1). The paper's 3x starts there.

Polynomial-matrix arithmetic would then be costed with #689, which wants the same subsystem.

## 8. Limits

- **No Zeebra timing was taken.** Every stage figure is #1039's, at `5cce19f0`; `main` has moved
  since, and §6.1's prediction assumes the operator stage is still what it was.
- **§6.1 is a prediction.** Reading the last operator off the basis is not implemented or measured
  here. The 59% and 33% are what the stage costs today, not what removing it returns.
- **The model drops constants on one side.** That is deliberate: it can refute, and does on the
  chain rows; on the dense rows the verdict rests on the paper's measured ratios, taken on another
  host with another library.
- **Anemoi and Griffin instances are not in this repository** (#668). §6.3 is from
  `griffin-freelunch.md`'s recorded sizes; the identification with Theorem 5.2 was checked against
  the lemma as that note states it, not against the FreeLunch paper's proof, and the paper's
  hypothesis `y ≺ x_i` was not checked against FreeLunch's weighted order.
- **`pquad-12-1`'s 45.7 s and 98 s** are single development attempts from #1039's exploration.
- **Faugère–Gaudry–Huot–Renault 2014 and Neiger–Schost** (the issue's neighbouring results) were
  not read for this note. BNS position their bound against both; `block-krylov-conversion.md`
  already rejects the block route on its own measurements.
