## 1. Findings

1. **The plan's Amdahl targets hold.** At 16 workers symbolic preprocessing is 31.7% of Eco-12's
   process wall, 34.2% of MQ10/2³¹−1's, 22.7% of MQ11's and 31.5% of Katsura-11's (median clocked
   stage over median unclocked wall; the same run's stage over its own F4 total is 2–4 points
   higher). A −10% process wall needs a stage speedup of **1.46× (Eco-12), 1.41× (MQ10/2³¹−1), 1.44×
   (MQ10/32003), 1.79× (MQ11) and 1.46× (Katsura-11)** — plan §8.2's 1.45 / 1.42 / 1.77 confirmed
   within 0.02. The ceiling (an instant stage) is −23% to −34% at 16 workers.
2. **At one worker the stage cannot pay for −10% anywhere** (*f* = 2.9–8.9%), and at four workers
   only MQ10, Eco-12 and Katsura-11 can (needing 1.75–2.26×). The opportunity exists only because
   forward elimination parallelizes and symbolic does not; it grows with the worker count.
3. **The closure is an interner.** Replaying the shipping closure part by part on every live batch,
   **74–81% of it is probing and inserting monomials into the hash interner**, 4–5% hashing them, 8–9%
   shifting terms and 1–8% divisor search (8% on Eco-12, whose basis is wide). Covered checks,
   seeding and the FIFO queue are under 2% together.
4. **The interner's cost is term volume, not column count.** Each column is probed 47 (MQ10) to 513
   (MQ11) times; 97.9–99.8% of probes hit a monomial already interned. The interner's own table
   peaks at 0.7–1.7 MiB per batch, so this is not a cache-capacity problem: it is ~28 ns per term
   for ~2.5 M (MQ10) to ~37 M (MQ11) terms.
5. **Every serial part but one is an implementation artefact (§4).** The algebraic dependency is the
   closure's *depth*: a monomial is discovered only after the row containing it exists, so the
   breadth-first levels are a true critical path. The FIFO order *within* a level, and the handle
   numbering, never reach the matrix: columns are sorted by monomial and reducer rows by leading
   column before elimination.
6. **#827's level-parallel prototype, re-priced on the shipping closure: 1.31× (MQ10), 2.01×
   (Eco-12), 1.17× (MQ11), 1.41× (Katsura-11) at 16 workers, 0.56–0.71× at one.** It is bounded by
   the serial interning of the seed rows' terms (56% of its MQ11 time) and by its commit (§5). As is,
   it is worth −3% to −16% of process wall; the cost model's hypothetical ≈4× is replaced in §6 by
   #860's measured −19% to −26%.

## 4. Serial parts: algebraic dependency or implementation artefact

| Serial part | Share | Classification | Why |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Interning (probe, insert, handle per term) | 74–81% | **artefact** | Handle numbers never reach the matrix: `build_measured` sorts columns by monomial and maps each handle through `column_of`, so any bijection gives the same matrix. Admitting each monomial once is a set operation; its order is free. |
| Hashing | 4–5% | artefact | Per term, independent. |
| Term shifting | 8–9% | artefact | Per row, independent. |
| Divisor search | 1–8% | artefact | A function of the monomial and the basis alone, under every `ReducerSelection` policy. |
| Covered check | 0–2% | artefact | A processed monomial is never queried again, so for every query "covered" means "is a pair lcm": a fixed, read-only set. |
| Seeding (pair halves, `Distinct` keys) | ≈0% | artefact | Per pair; the first-occurrence rule is a deterministic merge. |
| FIFO order within a level | — | artefact | The reducer *set* is the least fixed point of "add the selected reducer of every reachable uncovered monomial", which does not depend on discovery order; `partition_reducers_first` then orders reducer rows by leading column. Pair rows keep pair order, which the closure does not touch. |
| Row-budget refusal point | — | artefact | Because the row count is order-independent, "more than `max_rows` rows" is decided identically by checking after each level. Only the work done before refusing differs. |
| **Closure depth (breadth-first levels)** | — | **algebraic dependency** | A monomial exists only once the row containing it has been selected. The levels are a critical path of 29–121 steps per F4 call (§5); each needs the previous level's interned set. |
| `max_pending_monomials`, `monomials_enqueued` | — | diagnostic | Counters that depend on order but feed nothing. |

So the only dependency is the per-level barrier. Inside a level everything — including the commit
#827 left serial — can be distributed, provided the *output* equality the test suite checks
(`Preprocessed` field for field) is kept or relaxed to matrix equality, which §4 shows is sufficient.

## 5. The #827 level-parallel prototype on the post-M29 path

`symbolic_cost_model --replay level` replays every live batch both serially and level-parallel
(both seeding every pair half, since the prototype has no seed policy), asserts equality, and keeps
the prototype's per-level clocks. n = 5 per team size, all on CPUs 8–23; ms per F4 call.

(MQ10/32003 matches MQ10/2³¹−1 within 1%; teams 4 and 8 are in tables.md.)

What bounds it, in order:

1. **Seed-term interning, which #827 did not separate from the closure.** The seed rows' terms are
   interned serially before the first level: 27 ms of MQ10's 76, 192 of Eco-12's 531, and **725 of
   MQ11's 1 289** (56%). MQ11's batches are wide and shallow (23 levels in 12 batches), so most of its
   terms are seed terms; that is why the prototype barely moves it (1.17×).
2. **The serial commit**, 21–157 ms. Only 0–6% of reducer-row terms reach the commit's interner — the
   workers resolve the rest against the pre-level table — so the commit is mostly appending handles
   and rows, not interning.
3. **Restructuring at one worker** costs 1.4–1.8× (0.56–0.71×): every term is looked up by a worker
   and then copied again by the commit.
4. Synchronization is small at 16 workers (2–13 ms), but #827's static chunks leave the slowest
   slice well above the mean.

Against #859 §2's targets the prototype reaches the −10% stage speedup on Eco-12 only (2.01× vs
1.46×), and misses on MQ10 (1.31× vs 1.41×), Katsura-11 (1.41× vs 1.46×) and MQ11 (1.17× vs 1.79×).
#827's pre-dedup "two-thirds of the stage is the serial commit" becomes, on the post-M29 path, "the
serial part is the seed interning plus the commit", and both are interning-shaped: the #860
prototypes parallelize exactly these.

## 7. Method

- Five targets: `m29_cost_model`'s four M29 targets plus `katsura11-m31` (the published ladder row at
  2³¹−1, digest-checked). Shipping options (`F4Options::default()`: `PairMultiples::Distinct`, parallel
  candidate finishing, cache off); forward elimination serial at 1 worker, `Auto` capped at *w* above.
- Fresh process per child, `taskset` CCD-aligned from CPU 8 (one worker = CPU 8; 16 = 8–23),
  `tools/cpu-context.sh` before and after, arm order rotated per repetition; n = 5 per arm.
- Four separate suites, never pooled: **walls** (stage clocks off), **stages** (`--stages`),
  **components** and **level** (replay instruments). The replays run inside the observer before F4
  reduces each batch, on the live basis and pairs, assert their result equals the serial closure,
  and never feed back, so the trajectory is the live one; their process walls are not F4 walls.
- Component replay: the whole closure is timed before its parts on even batches and after them on
  odd ones. `hash` is the parts' standalone hash; `probe + insert` = (shift + intern) − (shift +
  hash). Allocation counts come from a counting global allocator in the instrument.
- Level replay: `symbolic_preprocessing_level_parallel_measured` (`research-work`) reads one clock per
  level and per worker slice; `sync` = dispatched levels' scan wall − their slowest slice.
- Deviations from the issue text: per-batch figures are reported as distributions of the replayed
  closure (§3), not of the live stage clock, which F4 records only in total; cache counters are
  unavailable (above).
