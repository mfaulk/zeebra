## 1. Question and rule

#945 proposes a dense forward-elimination kernel for small primes, from a serial profile of an
older Zeebra. #1033 asks whether that hotspot still exists on the **best current configuration**,
and whether it is dense where it is hot. The rule (pre-declared on #1033; plan §9.3) is fixed
before the read:

- **Opens #945** iff on both deciding rows (dense planted MQ over GF(31), n = 18 and 20):
  - F ≥ 0.60: elimination share of process wall;
  - D ≥ 0.50: share of products landing on rows at ≥ 50% span density;
  - R ≥ 1.5: best Zeebra at 1 worker over grobx at 1 worker.
- **Defers** on F < 0.40 or R ≤ 1.2 on either row. Anything in between also defers.
- **Narrows** to the D′ residual only if the best route is F4 and the trace confines fill there.

Two owner decisions were recorded before the read:

1. **n = 20 runs above the shipping live budget** (`--live-gib 8`). The shipping
   `F4Options::default()` (32 M entries / 512 MB) refuses it.
2. **The best route is the signature engine, not F4.** The deciding row is the `entry` dispatcher,
   which routes these rows to the signature engine. F and D are therefore measured on that route,
   and F4's D is reported beside it.

**Statistic note.** The pre-declaration calls R "paired". Zeebra and grobx attempts are not paired
repetitions of one binary, so R uses `tools/bench_stats.independent_ratio`, the Zeebra-vs-competitor
rule, which resamples each side independently. A 31× point estimate makes the choice immaterial to
the verdict.

**Session validity.** The first session's two slow jobs (`4b101574…`, `214b560f…`) were **void**:
13 of 15 and 5 of 18 attempts were contaminated, because other sessions' compilers started
mid-job. Publication mode checks for compilers only before a job's first attempt. Both void
jobs are kept as records, `summarize.py` excludes them whole, and their re-runs (`420abd54…`,
`5adf8f73…`) are clean: 0 of 15 and 0 of 18 contaminated. The fast jobs had 0 of 45 and 2 of 27
contaminated, and those 2 attempts are excluded.

## 4. Where the time goes (deliverable 1, phase attribution)

- **F4 at n = 18–20 is all forward pass.**
  - Serially, `stages.forward_ns` is 94.5% (n = 18) and 93.4% (n = 20) of wall.
  - Symbolic preprocessing, the backward pass and conversion together are under 7%.
  - One batch (degree 5) holds 95.3% (n = 18, 24863 × 13866) and 97.4% (n = 20, 37240 × 24262) of
    forward products.
- **The signature route is all elimination too.** `elimination_ns` is 98.2% of wall serially and
  80–81% at 16 workers. The remainder at 16 w is the serial parts of the parallel cascade.
- **Why `entry` wins.** It is 2.7× faster than F4 serially (64 s vs 172 s at n = 18) and
  parallelizes cleanly: 10.0× at 16 workers at n = 18, 10.5× at n = 20. F4 at the shipping budget
  falls back to serial at 16 workers (#1068), and its 4-worker read is bimodal for the same reason.
- **Neither route closes the gap.** grobx's serial run is still 3× faster than Zeebra's best
  16-worker configuration.

## 5. Density during reduction (deliverable 2)

Span density is a row's nonzeros at or right of its current lead, divided by the columns from the
lead to the last column. Width density (nonzeros / all columns) is reported alongside and decides
nothing. Each trace is one serial `--density` attempt. It covers 100.00% of the engine's own
coefficient products, so all four traces pass the 99% coverage check.

| Engine, row | Share of products on rows at span density ≥ 25% / ≥ 50% / ≥ 75% | Width ≥ 50% |
|---|---|---|
| signature, n = 18 | 0.9998 / **0.8994** / 0.4385 | 0.0256 |
| signature, n = 20 | 0.9996 / **0.9077** / 0.3187 | 0.0121 |
| F4, n = 18 | 0.9990 / **0.9679** / 0.6858 | 0.2393 |
| F4, n = 20 | 0.9995 / **0.9819** / 0.5560 | 0.2201 |

Rows fill quickly once reduction starts. In F4's dominant batch at n = 18, mean span density is
10.7% at intake, 38.5% after one reducer application, 52.9% after 16 and 62% after 64. The work is
dense in the sense a dense kernel exploits: the active tail of the row is mostly nonzero. The
fill is not confined to a residual block: it sits in the main forward pass of a single batch, which
is a second reason the gate opens rather than narrows. Width density stays low because a reduced
row's lead moves right. A kernel must therefore be dense over the active span, not over the full
width.

## 7. Delayed-reduction bounds (deliverable 4)

`research/1033-dense-rebaseline/bounds.py` computes, in exact arithmetic, how many products
(p − 1)² can be added to a reduced residue before an accumulator of each width must reduce:
λ = ⌊(2^γ − 1 − (p − 1)) / (p − 1)²⌋.

| Field | u16 | u32 | u64 | f64 (53-bit exact) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GF(31) | 72 | 4,772,185 | 2.0 × 10¹⁶ | 1.0 × 10¹³ |
| GF(32003) | — | 4 | 1.8 × 10¹⁰ | 8,794,993 |
| GF(2³¹ − 1) | — | — | 4 | — |
| Goldilocks, wide64 (2⁶⁴ − 353), 2⁶⁴ − 59 | — | — | — | — (u128: 1) |

GF(31) can delay reduction across a whole row in u32, and across 72 products even in u16. 64-bit
primes are covered by no f64 multiword or u64 lane.

- **The gap does not depend on the field.** GF(32003) matches GF(31) on every dense row: about 30×
  serially from n = 15 up. A GF(31)-only kernel would leave GF(32003) where it is. The bounds table

- **Planted vs unplanted makes no difference** at n = 15.
- **The gap grows with density and size.** The sparse controls are 2.3× (gf31 MQ10) and 11.9×

- **A selection finding, outside this gate.** `entry` routes gf31 mq-random-11 and dense n = 15 to

  - dense n = 15: 3.9 s against 2.5 s at 1 w.

  This is recorded on #1012 (selection headroom).

## 9. Consequences for #945

- **The target moved.** #945 must beat the **best route**, which is the signature engine through
  `entry` at 16 workers (owner decision 2), at complete-task level. Beating F4 alone is not enough.
  - On these rows that means 6.39 s (n = 18) and 53.66 s (n = 20).
  - The complete-task bar is a paired wall upper bound ≤ 0.87 against those.
- **The kernel shape the data supports:**
  - dense over the active span, since span density is high and width density low;
  - a u32 delayed-reduction lane, which serves both GF(31) and GF(32003);
  - applied to the signature engine's reduction workspace as well as F4's forward pass. The density
    hooks added here (`Workspace::reduce`) mark the place.
- **#1068 is independent and cheaper.** F4's 16-worker forward pass falls back to serial at the
  shipping live budget, which costs about 10× at n = 18.
- **Matrix-level gate.** #945's first gate (≥ 2× serial forward pass at n = 20, identical echelon
  form) can run directly on `gf31__mq-dense-20-s20__b3.zmat` (97.4% of forward products).
