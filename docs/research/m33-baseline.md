## 1. Sessions and contamination

One session per track, n = 9, arms interleaved and rotated per repetition, a fresh process per
attempt, 180 s and 8 GiB caps. Both first sessions were void, so each was re-run once, as the plan
requires. The **re-run is the session of record**, fixed in `summarize.py` before the re-runs were
read. The first sessions are kept as a replication (§5).

The thresholds are unchanged. The last three columns re-judge the same per-attempt metrics with the
same rules, grouped three ways: by one session median, by worker count (as #965 asked), and by arm
within each cell. The last grouping is a diagnostic only.

**What voids these sessions is the clock rule meeting different solvers in one group, not foreign
load.** In Track G the largest foreign share on the bench CPUs was 1.3% and the largest sibling
share 0.4%, against the 5% rules. Yet on Katsura-11 at 1 w the sampled bench clock reads 4.54 GHz
for every groebner-rs and conventional-F4 attempt, 3.99 GHz for the signature route and 2.67 GHz
for grobx. The cell median then lands on one solver and the others are flagged, which is why whole
arms drop out. Eco-12's nine grobx 0.155.1 attempts were all flagged in the first session, and no
Katsura-11 grobx attempt survives in the re-run. Grouped by arm, no session is void. This is the
calibration finding (#985) generalized: a group must not mix programs, not only worker counts.
Deciding that is the owner's call on #965, so every table below uses **all ok attempts**, and the
scoreboard also gives the ratio over rule-clean attempts only, with their counts. The two readings
agree wherever the rule leaves both arms data.

**Cost model.** *d* is the smallest time removed from every Zeebra attempt that brings the ratio's

close the gap.

What the model says:

- **Track G needs about 40% of wall removed on every development row.** No single stage closes it.
  Elimination alone would have to shrink by 70–78%, and symbolic cannot (212–594%). Eco-12 needs
  elimination −78% or a combination such as elimination −50% (686 ms) plus symbolic −50% (229 ms)
  plus the digest (87 ms) plus 62 ms more.
- **HG3 (≥ 5% non-algorithmic).** The digest is 3.4% of Eco-12's wall and 10.0% of Katsura-11's.
  The digest runs after the solve call. On Eco-12 the process wall less the call is 113 ms (4.4%):
  the 87 ms digest plus 26 ms of exec, input and teardown.
- **Track M's MQ10 needs about half its wall removed at msolve's best count.** That is a larger
  relative cut than Track G's, on a route with no stage clocks yet. M1's instrumentation comes first.

## 7. Peak RSS and observed threads

The full table is in `tables.md`. Highlights:

- **msolve** uses exactly the threads it is given (active = concurrent = 1/4/8/16). Its CPU ÷ wall
  at 16 w is 7.1–13.6, against 3.8–7.3 at 8 w. That is spinning, not parallel work, consistent
  with its 16-w slowdowns.
- **grobx** (both pins) computes on one thread. groebner-rs at "1 worker" shows two active threads.
- **Zeebra:**
  - entry at 16 w: CPU ÷ wall 3.5 on Eco-12, 3.1 on MQ11 and 2.1 on Katsura-11.
  - Katsura-11 at small primes: 1.5–2.1 at 4–16 w, the signature route's parallel cascade.
  - The census reads one active thread for several Zeebra multi-worker cells. Its threads start and
    exit between 50 ms samples, so the census is a lower bound there, and CPU ÷ wall is the evidence.
- **Peak RSS** (`wait4`): Zeebra 168 MiB on Eco-12, 364 MiB on MQ11 and 98–111 MiB on Katsura-11.
  Every solver is far below the 8 GiB cap. Readings near 24–31 MiB are a harness floor, not the
  solver's: msolve on Katsura-4 peaks at 5 MiB under `/usr/bin/time` but reads 31 MiB in the
  journal, because `wait4` reports the largest process in the waited tree, which includes the
  `rss-guard` wrapper. grobx's and msolve's peaks are therefore upper bounds until #990 is fixed.

## 8. Method notes

- **Arms.**
  - Track G: `sigf4`, `f4` and `entry` at 1 w; `entry` at 16 w; grobx 0.155.1 and 0.73.3 raw; and
    groebner-rs at 1 w.
  - Track M: `entry` and msolve at 1/4/8/16 w, plus `sigf4` at 1 w for H0.
  - Every Zeebra arm runs `--digest-only`. gf32003 / noon-7 runs with `--generate`, since it is in
    no catalog.
- **bench-job.** Three minimal extensions made the cohort expressible, each with a test in
  `tools/test/bench-job-test.py`: per-instance named inputs, per-arm worker lists, and
  `build.comparand`. The schema is in [`benchmark-schema.md`](benchmark-schema.md) §7. Large
  basis-text payloads are not committed; `journals/<job>/omitted.json` lists them by digest.
- **Statistics.** `tools/bench_stats.py`, imported verbatim from #979's branch (still open at
  writing), so no third bootstrap copy exists.
- **Sessions used:** 4 of #966's 5, plus an untimed thread-census pass and untimed references off
  the bench CPUs.
